Research Background & Retrieval Methodology

Women's health conditions involving gender-specific pain—such as dysmenorrhea, endometriosis, chronic pelvic pain, and vulvodynia—have historically been trivialized within biomedical frameworks or rendered invisible due to the lack of singular biomarker validation. The Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) community has increasingly addressed this by exploring how interactive systems represent embodied suffering, mediate self-care, and bridge the epistemic gap between patients and clinicians. This visualizer synthesizes key literature across flagship venues (ACM CHI, DIS, CSCW) to provide an interactive, structured mapping of the field's trajectory.

Temporal Evolution & Paradigm Shifts

HCI research on women's pain has undergone three major paradigms: moving from normative quantitative tracking to collaborative illness management, and now toward somatic, multi-sensory embodiment and radical rest.

Evolution of Primary Interaction Modalities Over Time

Early Phase (Mid–Late 2010s)

Personal Informatics & Normative Tracking

Focused on 28-day normative cycles and fertility. Reduced complex pain symptoms to numerical scale ratings (1–5), causing user alienation and feedback errors for irregular cycles.

Mid Phase (2018–2022)

Collaborative Illness Work & PGHD

Highlighted the invisible data labor of enigmatic conditions like endometriosis. Used machine learning for temporal phenotyping to bridge patient-clinician epistemic gaps.

Recent Phase (2023–Present)

Soma Design, Soft Robotics & Crip Theory

Critiques neoliberal productivity pressures and champions the right to rest. Implements knitted soft robotics, on-body pneumatic haptics, and cathartic voice AI.

3 Core Theoretical Debates in Women's Pain HCI

Three fundamental tensions define contemporary research, contrasting normative, medicalized approaches against critical, embodied, and feminist alternatives.

Debate 01

Linear Quantification vs. Embodied Subjective Sensation

Clash between traditional numerical pain scales (1–10 VAS) aimed at objective clinical control and emerging tactile/robotic externalizations of qualitative sensations (throbbing, heaviness).

Numerical Quantification: Data reductionism, input fatigue, erasure of qualitative nuances.
Somatic / Material Narrative: Externalizing visceral states via soft pneumatic inflation and tactile agency.
Debate 02

Productivity Maintenance vs. Radical Normalization of Rest

Conflict between neoliberal commercial FemTech designed to numb pain for continuous labor productivity versus Crip Theory advocating the legitimacy of stopping and resting.

Commercial FemTech Patches: Treating pain as an individual defect to conceal for workplace output.
Crip Theory / Tech for Rest: Acknowledging pain chronicity; Radical Normalization of Rest.
Debate 03

The Medical Gaze vs. Patient Agency & Self-Advocacy

Tension over diagnostic delays (7–10 years) where patient-generated health data (PGHD) is dismissed by clinicians as unstandardized, vs. empowering patients with self-advocacy tools.

Clinical Skepticism: Exclusion of unverified self-tracking; persistent pain invalidation.
Human-Centered AI (HAI): Temporal phenotyping and cathartic voice AI for patient agency.

Unexplored Research Gaps (4 Critical Gaps)

Despite recent theoretical advancements, several critical areas remain under-researched, presenting key opportunities for future HCI scholars.

Gap 01 Inclusivity Gap

Exclusion of Transgender & Non-Binary (TGD) Individuals

Most studies restrict samples to cisgender women. Transgender men and non-binary people undergoing Hormone Replacement Therapy (HRT) face pelvic pain and breakthrough bleeding, but are alienated by cis-normative, pink-aesthetic UI designs.

💡 Critical Design Question: How can non-normative interfaces support bodies experiencing intersecting gender dysphoria and chronic pelvic pain?
Gap 02 Stigma Gap

Intimate & Genital Pain (Vulvodynia & Dyspareunia)

Conditions like vulvar vestibulitis and pelvic floor dysfunction involve intense social stigma, loss of sexual pleasure, and relationship strain. Hardware and sensory interface research addressing intimate body pain remains exceptionally rare.

💡 Critical Design Question: How can interactive devices offer emotional and somatic validation for highly stigmatized intimate anatomy?
Gap 03 AI Ethics Gap

Pain Prediction & Catastrophizing Feedback Loops

Predictive AI models using voice or biometric signals to alert impending pain flare-ups may inadvertently prime users with fear and anxiety, triggering a psychological feedback loop that amplifies actual pain perception.

💡 Critical Design Question: How can predictive alerts provide reassurance rather than inducing pain catastrophizing?
Gap 04 Infrastructure Gap

Global South & Low-Resource Environments

Recent research disproportionately emphasizes costly smart textiles and pneumatic robotics. Open-source, low-cost appropriate tech for low-resource regions—where menstrual taboos and lack of hygiene infrastructure persist—is severely lacking.

💡 Critical Design Question: How can appropriate-technology HCI facilitate autonomous pain management in low-infrastructure contexts?
